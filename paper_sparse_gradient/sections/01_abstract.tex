\begin{abstract}
Sparse-index gradient accumulation is a recurring operation in backpropagation for embedding-heavy and sparse-feature AI training workloads. Although high-level tensor APIs may express related accumulation semantics, their CUDA execution paths can differ substantially in practice. This paper presents a controlled empirical study of three standard PyTorch CUDA paths for sparse-index accumulation: \texttt{index\_add}, \texttt{scatter\_add}, and \texttt{Embedding.backward}. The study uses a calibrated benchmark protocol with correctness validation, matched workload configurations, CUDA-event timing, a practical-margin rule for winner classification, representative profiler-based attribution, and a small training-step proxy.

The results show that the fastest API path is workload- and stack-dependent. In the matched benchmark phase, the local GTX1650Ti stack most often favored \texttt{Embedding.backward}, whereas the T4 stacks most often favored \texttt{scatter\_add}. Representative profiler evidence further shows that the three APIs dispatch to distinct CUDA kernel compositions: \texttt{index\_add} is dominated by indexed accumulation kernels, \texttt{scatter\_add} by scatter/gather-style accumulation kernels, and \texttt{Embedding.backward} by a multi-kernel backward path that includes accumulation and sorting or segmentation-related components. Strong representative reversals confirm that high-level API similarity does not guarantee equivalent performance behavior.

The training-step proxy reinforces this conclusion in a broader backward-style setting. On the local GTX1650Ti stack, the proxy most often favored the custom \texttt{index\_add} backward path, whereas the T4 stack was dominated by practical ties and its resolved cases mainly favored \texttt{scatter\_add}. These findings indicate that sparse-index gradient accumulation should be treated as a workload-, timing-scope-, and software/hardware-stack-dependent systems problem rather than as a purely semantic API choice. The study does not claim a universal fastest API, isolated hardware causality, or production training throughput; instead, it provides a reproducible characterization of standard PyTorch CUDA paths under controlled sparse-gradient accumulation workloads.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} sparse gradients; sparse-index accumulation; backpropagation; PyTorch; CUDA; embedding backward; index add; scatter add; AI model training
