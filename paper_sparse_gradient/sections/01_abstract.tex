\begin{abstract}
Sparse-index gradient accumulation appears in embedding-heavy and sparse-feature training workloads, but semantically related PyTorch CUDA APIs need not have interchangeable performance. This study characterizes three standard paths, \texttt{index\_add}, \texttt{scatter\_add}, and \texttt{Embedding.backward}, using correctness-gated benchmarks, matched workload grids, CUDA-event timing, profiler inspection, and a small backward-style proxy.

The results show workload-, scope-, and stack-dependent behavior across the tested stacks. In the matched isolated benchmark, the local GTX1650Ti stack most often favored \texttt{Embedding.backward}, whereas the Colab T4 stack and its repeat run most often favored \texttt{scatter\_add}. Profiler summaries show distinct CUDA execution-path compositions. In the proxy, the local stack most often favored the custom \texttt{index\_add} backward path, while T4 observations were predominantly practical ties, with resolved cases mainly favoring \texttt{scatter\_add}. Isolated rankings therefore do not transfer mechanically to the broader proxy scope.

Threshold-sensitivity checks, descriptive bootstrap intervals, and winner maps contextualize practical ties and workload-specific patterns. Descriptive regret analysis quantifies the cost of static API selection: the lowest-mean-regret static choice varies by stack and scope, and even \texttt{index\_add}, which has the smallest observed worst-case static regret, reaches 145.0\%. This does not support a safe universal default, and ties among leading paths do not make all candidates interchangeable. The practical output is a reproducible performance characterization and measurement-backed interpretation framework, culminating in a tie-aware selection protocol for the target workload and execution stack.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} sparse gradients; sparse-index accumulation; backpropagation; PyTorch; CUDA; embedding backward; index add; scatter add; AI model training
