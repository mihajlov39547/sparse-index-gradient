\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented a reproducible performance characterization of three standard PyTorch CUDA sparse-index accumulation paths: \texttt{index\_add}, \texttt{scatter\_add}, and \texttt{Embedding.backward}. Matched controlled workloads showed workload-, scope-, and stack-dependent behavior, with no universal fastest API in the saved observations. The GTX1650Ti stack most often favored \texttt{Embedding.backward} in the isolated grid, whereas the hosted Colab T4 stack and its repeat run most often favored \texttt{scatter\_add}. The proxy instead favored \texttt{index\_add} on GTX1650Ti and was tie-heavy on T4, showing that isolated rankings do not transfer mechanically to a broader backward-style scope.

Profiler summaries showed distinct CUDA execution-path compositions. The direct APIs were dominated by their corresponding accumulation kernels, while \texttt{Embedding.backward} included accumulation and sorting or segmentation-related components. This evidence supports execution-path interpretation within the tested stacks, without isolating GPU architecture as the cause.

The descriptive threshold, bootstrap, winner-map, and regret analyses refine this interpretation. Static selection can be costly, and the lowest-mean-regret static choice is stack- and scope-dependent. Although \texttt{index\_add} has the smallest observed worst-case static regret across the saved observations, its maximum remains 145.0\%, so it is not a safe universal default. Practical ties among leading paths do not make all candidate APIs interchangeable.

The practical output is a measurement-backed, tie-aware selection protocol: measure candidate paths under the target workload and stack, retain small differences as practical ties, check isolated rankings against a broader backward-style scope when relevant, and use profiler evidence to inspect execution-path divergence. This guidance is limited to controlled workloads, diagnostic profiling cases, two GPU/software stacks, and a synthetic proxy. Fuller training loops, real traces, additional stacks, sparse optimizer behavior, and lower-level profiling remain future work.
